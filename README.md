## 1. Data Overview

This Power BI project focuses on HR analytics and aims to provide insights into various aspects of human resources within an organization. The dataset used for analysis contains several columns related to employee information as stated below:

* EmpID: Employee ID.
* Age: Age of the employee.
* AgeGroup: Age group to which the employee belongs.
* Attrition: Attrition status (whether the employee has left the organization or is still active). 
* BusinessTravel: Frequency of business travel of employee.
* Department: Department in which the employee works.
* DistanceFromHome: Distance in miles from the employee's home to the workplace. 
* EducationField: Field of education of the employee. 
* EnvironmentSatisfaction: Employee's satisfaction level with the work environment .
* Gender: Gender of the employee. 
* JobLevel: Level of the employee's job position .
* JobRole: Role of the employee within the organization.
* JobSatisfaction: Employee's satisfaction level with their job.
* MonthlyIncome: Monthly income of the employee. 
* MaritalStatus: Marital status of the employee. 
* SalarySlab: Categorization of monthly income into salary slabs. 
* MonthlyRate: Monthly rate of pay for the employee. 
* OverTime: Whether the employee works overtime or not. 
* PercentSalaryHike: Percentage increase in salary for the employee. 
* RelationshipSatisfaction: Employee's satisfaction level with work relationships.
* TotalWorkingYears: Total number of years the employee has worked. 
* WorkLifeBalance: Employee's work-life balance satisfaction level. 
* YearsAtCompany: Number of years the employee has worked at the current company.
* YearsInCurrentRole: Number of years the employee has been in the current role. 
* YearsSinceLastPromotion: Number of years since the employee's last promotion. 
* YearsWithCurrManager: Number of years the employee has been working with the current manager. 

The primary objective is to derive data-driven insights by calculating critical metrics such as:

* Overall employee attrition rate
* Age group with highest attrition rate
* Impact of salary level on employee attrition
* Effect of distance from home on attrition
* Correlation of overtime with attrition
* Attrition trend over time

Through advanced data analysis and visualization techniques, this project aims to provide actionable insights that support informed decision-making to enhance employee engagement and organizational perfromance.

# 3. EDA - Exploratory data analysis Key Insights
1️ Age Group With Highest Attrition Rate
* Employees aged 18–25 have the highest attrition rate (~35%), indicating early-career professionals are more likely to leave.
* 26–35 and 55+ age groups show moderate attrition, suggesting mid-career and near-retirement transitions.
* Attrition is lowest among 36–45, indicating higher job stability in this segment.

## 4.Insights and Report


## Employee Attrition - Insights & Trends
* High Attrition Among Young Employees: The 18–25 age group records the highest attrition rate, indicating early-career professionals are more likely to switch jobs.
* Stable Retention in Mid-Career Employees: Employees aged 36–45 show lower attrition, suggesting higher job satisfaction and career stability.
* Salary-Based Attrition: Employees earning up to 5k have the highest attrition rate, reflecting dissatisfaction with pay or limited growth opportunities.
* Income Stability Impact: Attrition decreases significantly in higher salary slabs (15k+), showing compensation plays a key role in retention.
* Distance Influence: Attrition slightly increases with longer commuting distances, suggesting that convenience and work-location proximity affect employee decisions.
* Overtime Burnout: Employees working overtime experience higher attrition (approx. 30%), indicating workload imbalance and potential burnout.
* Tenure Patterns: Attrition is highest during the first few years of employment, declines mid-career, and rises again near retirement.
* Retention Focus: Strengthening onboarding, engagement programs, and flexible work policies can improve retention rates.
* Compensation & Work-Life Balance: Fair pay structures and reduced overtime workloads can improve overall satisfaction.



# Overall Conclusion for the HR Analytics Dashboard

  The HR Analytics Dashboard provides a comprehensive view of workforce attrition and employee demographics. The analysis highlights key factors influencing 
  employee turnover, including age, salary, commute distance, overtime, and years of service. These insights help HR teams design data-driven strategies to improve retention and job satisfaction.

## Key Takeaways:
* Attrition Rate: The overall attrition stands at 16.12%, primarily driven by younger and lower-income employees.
* Age Insights: Early-career employees are the most likely to leave, requiring focused engagement and mentorship programs.
* Salary Influence: Compensation has a direct link with attrition — higher pay correlates with greater employee stability.
* Workload Impact: Overtime significantly contributes to attrition, reflecting the importance of work-life balance.
* Tenure Analysis: High early-exit rates indicate the need to strengthen employee onboarding and growth pathways.
* Distance Factor: Employees with longer commutes show moderate attrition; hybrid work options can improve satisfaction.

# Final Recommendations:
✅ Enhance Early-Career Retention – Provide career growth programs, mentorship, and onboarding support for new hires.

✅ Revise Compensation Structures – Ensure competitive and transparent pay scales to reduce low-income attrition.

✅ Improve Employee Engagement – Conduct regular feedback surveys and wellness initiatives to maintain motivation.

✅ Promote Work-Life Balance – Limit overtime, encourage flexible schedules, and support hybrid work arrangements.
